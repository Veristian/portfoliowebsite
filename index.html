02 / EXPERIENCE

My Journey

This is my journey as a Game Programmer, showcasing the projects I've worked on and the skills I've developed along the way as well as some nifty things I personally like about each project.

01
2D Platformer Rhythm

Dissonant Melody

Dissonant Melody was my first major undertaking after learning Unity. I was interested in rhythm games at the time and wanted to incorporate it into another favourite genre of mine: platformers. I designed a system that had players use movement abilities in time with notes so players have to jump and run in position and time their abilities to the beat. This forces players to think about their movement and timing in a different way than a traditional platformer since you won't be able to stop or use your abilities at any time.

What I learned

One of the things I focused on was creating good movement feel to the game, trying to incorporate coyote time and jump buffering like most good platformers. It felt quite novel trying to program something as abstract as good game feel and I learned the importance of game polish after ocmpleting the project.

02
Game Jam 3D Platformer Difficult

Time to Meownch

Time to Meownch was my first game jam (Indie Jam 2025) exprience. The theme of the jam was "Eat" and though discussion we decided to make a cute cat platformer where players have to eat food as a rolling ball cat to get bigger which allows them to jump higher and further. They can also fart to propel themselves in the air but farting makes the cat smaller which reduces their jumping ability. I was responsible for programming the movement mechanics and platform mechanics.

What I learned

This project taught me how to learn new things quickly and adapt under time constraints. Time to Meownch was able to earn 3rd place in the game jam and this also gave me confidence to not be afraid of delving into new things.

03
Mobile Resource Management Chaotic

Clocked In

Clocked In is a mobile resource management game where players have to manage their sanity by hopping between minigames and work while also getting disturbed by random events. This game makes use of controls unique to mobile like gyroscope and volume buttons for parts of the game. Gyroscope was used to control which direction the player is looking in the game and the volume buttons were used for shooting lasers in one of the minigames.

What I learned

This game project taught me about mobile game development as well as utilizing different programming languages to achieve specific functionalities.

04
Game Jam Atmospheric Escape

The Last Light

The Last Light was my third game jam experience (Indie Jam 2026). The theme of the jam was "Games for Change" and we decided to make an 2D atmospheric escape game where players have to escape a dark and dangerous cave as an anglerfish with limited visibility with pollution that hurts you while being hunter by a big predator.

What I learned

This game project taught me the importance of having a Game Design Document (GDD) as it helped me program efficiently by knowing exactly what I needed to do and the scope of the project. This allowed me to implement all mechanics on the first day, giving me more time for bug testing, incorporating assets from my teammates to the level, and adding atmospheric polish to the game, such as audio crossfades, particle effects, and etc.

05
Potion Crafting Puzzle Cute

A'ea: Alchemy's Odyssey

A'ea: Alchemy's Odyssey is a puzzle game where players have to craft potions by combining ingredients in the correct phase to create a wave matching that of the selected potion. Creating a potion does not limit the player to one set of ingredients for each recipe, as players can even create a potion with a different combinations of ingredients than intended as long as it matches the required wave. I was responsible for programming the wave based crafting system, the invetory system, the questing and dialogue system as well as manage how the game saves the player data and progress.

What I learned

This project I had three major undertakings, which were creating the wave-based crafting system that can draw waves dynamically, which can also bounce like viscous fluids. The second is managing the player's inventory and progression data effectively, so that the player's progress is saved and loaded correctly between playthroughs. Lastly, is the questing system, involving checking requirements to complete and unlock quests.
